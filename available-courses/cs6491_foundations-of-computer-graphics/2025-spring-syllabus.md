# CS 6491 — Spring 2025 Syllabus

Source: `downloads/CS 6491 2025-1.pdf` (OMSCS sample syllabus, Spring 2025; the PDF header mistypes the code as "CS 6451"). Creator and Instructor: Jeff Wilson, PhD.

## Overview

The course explores the mathematical, physical, and perceptual principles behind computer graphics, focusing on techniques for creating, representing, and displaying three-dimensional shapes and their properties. Designed for students with diverse backgrounds, it includes a brief review of fundamental concepts. The curriculum covers Image Synthesis (first half, emphasizing ray tracing principles and implementation) and 3D Modeling (second half).

## Sample Topics

3D graphics geometry basics; introduction to ray tracing; ray–object intersections and optimization (bounding volumes, spatial hierarchies, instancing); reflection and refraction (Fresnel); radiometry and BRDFs; distribution ray tracing; tone mapping and sampling; the rendering equation (Monte Carlo, importance sampling); texture mapping and procedural texturing (Perlin noise); implicit surfaces and ray marching; geometric primitives and meshes; mesh smoothing and subdivision; distance transforms; and GPU ray tracing.

## Grading

| Weight | Component |
|---|---|
| 80% | Programming Assignments (~five projects: ray tracing, implicit surfaces, mesh manipulation, etc.) |
| 20% | Module Quizzes |

All graded assessments are individual effort (no collaborative work).

- Pharr, Jakob & Humphreys, *Physically Based Rendering*, 3rd ed. ([pbr-book.org](https://pbr-book.org/))

## Technical Requirements

Students must own an approved Windows or macOS laptop. The course uses Java and the Processing graphics library. Late policy: 24-hour grace period with no penalty; no later submissions without Dean of Students approval.
