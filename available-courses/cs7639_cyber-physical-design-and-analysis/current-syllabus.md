## Overview

Cyber-physical systems comprise both a physical part and a software part: the physical part reports information about itself to the software, and the software sends commands back. Their development requires competencies in both physical and software systems. Because physical systems can harm operators (airplanes, medical devices, cars) or be costly to repair (power grids, centrifuges), the controlling software cannot rely on trial and error. The course introduces tools and models for developing high confidence in proper operation prior to operational testing — including model-based systems engineering and CPS verification and validation in current industry use, with examples from aerospace, automotive, and medical devices, and in-depth discussion of human operators. Various verification and validation formalisms (formal methods) are described and applied to simple examples.

## Prerequisites

A Computer Science or Engineering bachelor's or equivalent degree.

## Computer Languages / Software

The class mixes physical systems, signal processing, program specification, and programming. Engineering specification languages: AADL and Simulink. Programming languages: MATLAB and C. AADL is taught explicitly. Students without these backgrounds who are willing to learn have also succeeded.
