## Description

The course starts with an overview of Internet architecture and its evolution, then broadly covers protocols and algorithms spanning all layers of the Internet protocol stack, such as TCP and congestion control. It studies intradomain/interdomain routing, peering and network relationships, router design and functionalities, Software Defined Networking, the intersection of network security and computer networks (especially attacks on Internet routing such as BGP hijacking), and finally multimedia applications and Content Delivery Networks. Hands-on projects build understanding of routing, SDN, BGP hijacking, and Internet measurements.

## Prerequisites

A prior networking course is not required, but this is an advanced course: introductory undergraduate material is covered only at a review level. A prior understanding of basic networking concepts is expected (IP addresses, subnets, TCP vs. UDP, the OSI model and encapsulation, three-way handshakes, basic HTTP, client-server vs. peer-to-peer). Students are expected to code in Python at an intermediate level.

## Course Materials

No required textbook. Exams are based on the lectures and provided readings. Optional reference: *Computer Networking: A Top-Down Approach* by Kurose & Ross, 7th edition (ISBN-13: 978-0133594140). Course content (video lectures, readings) and all logistics are in Canvas; discussion is in Ed Discussion.

## Course Virtual Machine

Projects are released and graded on a VM. The recommended platform is VMware Player (free); Intel and ARM images are provided. Other virtualization packages (Parallels Desktop, VirtualBox, UTM) may be used with the base images. Building a custom VM is not recommended. Download links and setup instructions are in Ed Discussion.
