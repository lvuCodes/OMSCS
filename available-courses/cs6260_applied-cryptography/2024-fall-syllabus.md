## Course Description and Goals

A graduate-level introduction to modern cryptography focusing on the classical goals of cryptography — data privacy, authenticity, and integrity. It applies theory to practical problems but remains a theory course. Students learn what schemes such as AES, CBC, RSA, DSA, and TLS are and how they work, but the central objectives are more fundamental: understanding what "secure" means, how to evaluate and measure security, how to compare the security of schemes, and how to select parameters for required security guarantees. Internal block-cipher construction details are out of scope.

## Prerequisites

No previous cryptography knowledge is required. The main requirement is basic mathematical maturity — the ability to read and write mathematical definitions, statements, and proofs. Students are expected to have succeeded in undergraduate discrete math and taken basic algorithms and computability/complexity courses, including measuring algorithm running time and doing proofs by contradiction and contraposition, plus very basic probability. All necessary number theory is presented during the course.

## Course Materials

Lecture slides and video lectures are the primary source (some slides by Mihir Bellare). The Bellare–Rogaway (BR) lecture notes serve as the (slightly outdated) textbook and are highly recommended. Optional supplements: Boneh–Shoup draft, Nigel Smart's book, and Katz–Lindell, *Introduction to Modern Cryptography*.
