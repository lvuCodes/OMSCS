## 1 — Introduction

This course is a graduate-level course in the design and analysis of algorithms. We study techniques for the design of algorithms (such as dynamic programming) and algorithms for fundamental problems. In addition, we study computational intractability, specifically, the theory of NP-completeness. The main topics covered include: dynamic programming; divide and conquer; randomized algorithms, including RSA cryptosystem; graph algorithms; max-flow algorithms; linear programming; and NP-completeness.

Note: the syllabus and course schedule are subject to change. Any changes after the semester begins will be announced to students on Canvas or Ed Discussions.

### 1.1 — Textbook

The required textbook is *Algorithms* by S. Dasgupta, C. Papadimitriou, and U. Vazirani, frequently referred to as "DPV." The textbook *Algorithm Design* by J. Kleinberg and E. Tardos is also recommended.

## 4 — Assignments and Deliverables

### 4.1 — Homework

There will be homework assignments throughout the semester (written and/or coding). Homework is a learning opportunity and practice for exams; grades and feedback are for edification only and do not contribute to a student's grade. Homework is posted on Canvas and announced in Ed Discussions with a discussion thread. Each assignment is available for a week and due at 8am ET the following Monday, unless announced otherwise. There are no extensions except for approved emergencies or disability accommodations coordinated with staff.

Written homework has practice problems (ungraded) and graded problems, clearly marked. Practice solutions are released mid-week (typically Thursday), and the previous week's graded problem is discussed in office hours. Suggested problems may be posted separately (no solutions). Coding homework uses Python; solutions are not provided.

For all homework, students may: use official course materials without citation (lectures, textbook, posts on Ed Discussions or Ed Chat); collaborate with other currently enrolled students up to sharing entire solutions and test cases; consult any other references (including online and solutions manuals); use AI tools to help compose a solution; and reuse their old solutions from a previous iteration without citation. Sources and collaborators must be cited at the end of solutions. All submitted work must represent the student's own effort.

Students may not share any assignments or materials, or make them accessible in any way, with individuals not currently enrolled in CS 6515.

### 4.2 — Quizzes

There will be several Format and Content quizzes. Format quizzes show the elements of a complete, correct, efficient solution for a problem type; Content quizzes reinforce understanding and mimic autograded exam questions. These are mandatory, graded, delivered on Canvas, with attempt/scoring details posted per quiz on Ed Discussions.

Students may use official course materials (textbook, lectures, supplemental staff posts), personally generated notes, and the associated Ed Discussions thread for clarifying questions. Students may not discuss a quiz's questions or contents while it is open, or share its contents/questions/scope with anyone not enrolled.

Logistics Quizzes ensure awareness of policies and expectations:

- **Course Communication & Logistics Quiz** — how the class is run
- **Academic Integrity Quiz** — collaboration and plagiarism expectations
- **Onboarding Exam (graded)** — a chance to test the environment against the unique Exam Proctoring requirements and confirm understanding of those requirements

These are mandatory and together worth 3% of the final grade, with unlimited attempts (incorrect answers marked) until due.

### 4.3 — Exams

Exams are administered on Canvas through Honorlock. Exams are closed book, with no additional devices (no calculators, phones, or other applications) and no additional references (no notes or books). Students may take an exam any time within its window. There are no extensions under any circumstances; emergencies are handled case-by-case via alternate methods.

Full proctoring requirements are posted on Ed Discussions; items to review in advance:

- Review [Honorlock minimum system requirements](https://honorlock.kb.help/minimum-system-requirements/):
  - Broadband internet connection required.
  - Webcam and microphone required.
  - Honorlock does not support Linux OS, virtual machines, tablets, or smartphones.
  - Honorlock requires the Honorlock Chrome extension with Google Chrome.
- A secure, private location to take an exam.
- A picture I.D. provided as part of the exam process.
- A thorough room scan of the test environment.

More details, including a practice exam to mimic the technical environment and format, are released the week of the first exam. For proctoring violations, the staff reserve the right to enforce penalties and/or disqualify the exam. For poor proctoring, stricter standards may be enforced for all future exams (including future semesters of this course).

### 4.4 — Regrade Policy

Regrades ensure students receive the grade they deserve on manually graded assignments. A request must be made "in good faith" to correct a grading mistake and/or request clarification on a penalty, complying with the process in Ed. A regrade can result in an overall lower grade if a larger error is found or the process is abused.

## 5 — Academic Integrity Policy

Plagiarism, unauthorized collaboration, cheating in any form, and sharing course materials and assignments outside class are academic integrity violations and violate the [GT Honor Code](https://policylibrary.gatech.edu/student-life/academic-honor-code). All submissions are checked. Violations are reported to the GT Office of Student Integrity with a 0 on that grade component (OSI may impose stricter penalties for prior offenses).

Students may not share course materials, assignments, questions, solutions, content from Ed Chat or Ed Discussions, or any other sensitive material outside the class at any time. This applies to previous students, students who dropped, prospective students, public forums, Slack/Discord/WeChat/Reddit/etc., and cheating websites (Chegg/Course Hero/etc.). Discovered illegitimately shared materials should be reported to staff. The staff reserve the right to enforce additional penalties and/or drop a student's letter grade.

## 6 — Collaboration Policy

Authorized student collaboration is highly encouraged and historically an early indicator of success.

"Public" refers to any space, online or physical, accessible to someone not currently enrolled. Public discussions of course concepts (e.g., how RSA works, or whether P=NP) and publicly/legitimately available materials (textbook, lectures) are permitted unless directly related to an "open" assignment.

**Permitted Non-public Collaboration:**

| Activity | Homework | Quiz (open) | Quiz (closed) | Exam (during window) | Exam (after close) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Discussion of useful resources that might help | Yes | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |
| Discussion of questions and solutions | Yes | No | Yes | No | Yes |
| Sharing solutions | Yes | No | Yes | No | Yes |
| General discussion regarding CS 6515 topics | Yes | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |
| Use of AI tools | Yes | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |

Students are strongly recommended to work out their own solutions and write them up separately. Collaboration does not remove the requirement to cite and give credit.

Study groups are encouraged but not required, with no limit on how many a student joins; groups are capped at 30 members. A group of only currently enrolled students is "private"; one containing withdrawn members, former students, etc. is "public."

**Special note for repeat students:** Students who previously enrolled are prohibited from sharing any semester-specific questions, solutions, or materials from a previous semester with anyone not part of that offering. Repeat students may refer to those materials for their own personal studies.
