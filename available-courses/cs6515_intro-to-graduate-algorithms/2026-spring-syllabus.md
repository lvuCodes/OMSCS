# CS 6515: Introduction to Graduate Algorithms — Spring 2026

Source: `downloads/CS 6515 2026-1.pdf` ([omscs.gatech.edu/sites/.../CS 6515 2026-1.pdf](https://omscs.gatech.edu/sites/default/files/documents/Syllabi/CS%206515%202026-1.pdf))

## 1 — Introduction

This course is a graduate-level course in the design and analysis of algorithms. We study techniques for the design of algorithms (such as dynamic programming) and algorithms for fundamental problems. In addition, we study computational intractability, specifically, the theory of NP-completeness. The main topics covered in the course include: dynamic programming; divide and conquer; randomized algorithms, including RSA cryptosystem; graph algorithms; max-flow algorithms; linear programming; and NP-completeness.

Note: the syllabus and course schedule are subject to change after the semester begins. Changes, if any, will be announced to students via Canvas or Ed Discussions.

### 1.1 — Textbook

The required textbook is *Algorithms* by S. Dasgupta, C. Papadimitriou, and U. Vazirani, frequently referred to as "DPV" (initials of the authors). The textbook *Algorithm Design* by J. Kleinberg and E. Tardos is also recommended.

The textbook chapters are mapped to the lectures via "The Reading Index," a separate official course document found on Canvas. If a lecture is required, the associated textbook reading is also required.

## 4 — Assignments and Deliverables

### 4.1 — Homework

There will be homework assignments throughout the semester, consisting of written and/or coding components. Homework is designed to be a learning opportunity and practice for the exams; grades and feedback on homework are for student edification only and do not contribute to a student's grade. Homework is posted on Canvas and announced in Ed Discussions with an associated discussion thread. Each assignment is available for a week and due at 8am ET on the following Monday, unless announced otherwise. There are no extensions except for approved emergency situations or disability accommodations coordinated with course staff.

Written homework assignments have a set of practice problems (ungraded, not submitted) and graded problems (optionally submitted for grading), clearly marked. Solutions for practice problems are released mid-week (typically Thursday), and solutions to the previous week's graded problem are discussed in office hours. Suggested problems may also be posted separately as extra practice (no provided solutions). Written homework submissions are uploaded to Gradescope, typed (not handwritten) and properly tagged via Gradescope's submission features.

Coding homework assignments use Python to implement algorithm concepts. Solutions are not provided for these.

For all homework assignments, students may:

- use official course materials without citation (lectures, textbook, posts on Ed Discussions/Chat)
- collaborate with other currently enrolled students, up to and including sharing entire solutions and test cases
- consult any other references (including online and solutions manuals)
- use AI tools to help with composition of a solution
- reuse their old solutions from a previous iteration of the class without citation

Sources and collaborators must be cited at the end of solutions. All submitted work must represent the student's own effort.

Students may not share any assignments or materials (to include AI tools trained on course data), or make them accessible in any way, with individuals not currently enrolled in CS 6515. This includes students who were formerly enrolled but have since withdrawn, students from previous semesters, and private tutors.

### 4.2 — Quizzes

There will be several Format and Content quizzes during the semester. Format quizzes show the important elements of a complete, correct, and efficient solution for a particular type of problem. Content quizzes help students test and reinforce understanding and mimic autograded questions on exams. These quizzes are mandatory, graded, and delivered on Canvas. Details for number of attempts, scoring, etc. are posted on Ed Discussions in a separate thread per quiz.

Students may use official course materials (textbook, lectures, supplemental staff posts on Ed Discussions), use personally generated notes, and use the associated Ed Discussions thread to ask clarifying questions. Students may not discuss the questions or contents of a quiz while it is open for submission, or share the contents, questions, or scope with anyone not currently enrolled.

Logistics Quizzes ensure students are aware of policies and expectations:

- **Course Communication & Logistics Quiz** — how the class is run (communication methods, submission, scope, etc.)
- **Academic Integrity Quiz** — expectations on collaboration and plagiarism
- **Onboarding Exam** — a chance to test the environment against the unique Exam Proctoring requirements and confirm understanding of those requirements. A separate, ungraded Mock Exam is made available after the Onboarding Exam deadline to allow environment testing for the rest of the semester.

These are mandatory and together worth 3% of the final grade. There are unlimited attempts at each logistics quiz (with incorrect answers marked) until they are due.

### 4.3 — Exams

Exams are administered on Canvas through Honorlock. Exams are closed book, with no additional devices (no calculators, phones, or other applications) and no additional references (no notes or books). Students may take an exam at any time within its exam window. There are no extensions under any circumstances; emergency situations are handled case-by-case with course staff via alternate methods.

**New for Spring 2026 — Side Angle Proctoring.** Full proctoring requirements are posted on Ed Discussions; items to review in advance:

- Review [Honorlock minimum system requirements](https://honorlock.kb.help/minimum-system-requirements/):
  - Broadband internet connection required.
  - Webcam and microphone required.
  - Honorlock does not support Linux OS, virtual machines, tablets, or smartphones.
  - Honorlock requires the Honorlock Chrome extension with Google Chrome.
- A secure, private location to take an exam.
- A picture I.D. provided as part of the exam process.
- A thorough room scan of the test environment.
- [Side Angle Proctoring](https://honorlock.kb.help/setting-up-a-side-angle-camera/) for all exams, requiring a camera (external webcam or compatible smartphone setup), and possibly a tripod and/or extension cable. Not all webcams are compatible.
- The [Honorlock Desktop App](https://honorlock.kb.help/honorlock-application-test-taker-experience/) installed.

More details, including a mock exam to mimic the technical environment and format, are released the week of the first exam.

For proctoring violations (sessions failing to meet standards), the staff reserve the right to enforce penalties and/or disqualify the exam, independent of pursuing an academic integrity case with OSI. For poor proctoring, stricter standards may be enforced for all future exams (including future semesters of this course).

During an exam window, all discussion of CS 6515 content is prohibited — including in study groups, with a private tutor, or with former students. This applies to the entire exam window, not just the specific attempt block; failure to comply can be considered an academic integrity violation.

### 4.4 — Regrade Policy

A regrade request can result in an overall lower grade if a larger error is discovered, or as a consequence of abusing the regrade process.

## 5 — Academic Integrity Policy

Plagiarism (passing off outside work as one's own), unauthorized collaboration, cheating in any form, and sharing course materials and assignments outside of class are academic integrity violations and violate the [GT Honor Code](https://policylibrary.gatech.edu/student-life/academic-honor-code). All submissions are subject to checks. Violations are reported to the GT Office of Student Integrity, with a 0 on that grade component (OSI may impose stricter penalties for prior offenses).

Students may not share course materials, assignments, questions, solutions, content from Ed Chat or Ed Discussions, AI tools trained on course materials, or any other sensitive material outside the class at any time. This applies to previous students, students who dropped, prospective students, public forums, Slack/Discord/WeChat/Reddit/etc., and cheating websites (Chegg/Course Hero/etc.). Students who discover illegitimately shared materials should report the issue to course staff.

For academic integrity violations, the staff reserve the right to enforce additional penalties (such as disqualification from extra credit) and/or drop a student's letter grade.

## 6 — Collaboration Policy

Authorized student collaboration is highly encouraged and historically an early indicator of whether a student will succeed in the course.

"Public" refers to any space, online or physical, accessible to someone not currently enrolled. Public discussions of course concepts (e.g., how RSA works, or whether P=NP) and publicly/legitimately available materials (textbook, lectures) are permitted as long as they are not directly related to an "open" assignment.

**Permitted Non-public Collaboration:**

| Activity | Homework | Quiz (open) | Quiz (closed) | Exam (during window) | Exam (after close) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Discussion of useful resources that might help | Yes | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |
| Discussion of questions and solutions | Yes | No | Yes | No | Yes |
| Sharing solutions | Yes | No | Yes | No | Yes |
| General discussion regarding CS 6515 topics | Yes | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |
| Use of AI tools | Yes | Yes | Yes | No | Yes |

While homework collaboration rules are purposefully lenient, students are strongly recommended to work out their own solutions and write them up separately for the greatest learning benefit. Collaboration does not remove the requirement to cite and give credit; failure to do so can be considered plagiarism.

Study groups are encouraged but not required, with no limit to how many a student joins; groups are capped at 30 members. A study group consisting only of currently enrolled students is "private"; one containing members who have withdrawn, former students, private tutors, etc. is "public."

**Special note for repeat students:** Students who previously enrolled in CS 6515 are prohibited from sharing any semester-specific questions, solutions, or materials from a previous semester (including AI tools trained on course material) with anyone not part of that offering. Repeat students may refer to those materials for their own personal studies.
