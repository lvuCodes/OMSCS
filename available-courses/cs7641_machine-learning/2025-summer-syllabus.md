## General Information

Machine Learning is a three-credit course on the study and application of the field of Machine Learning — the area of Artificial Intelligence concerned with computational artifacts that modify and improve their performance through experience. Topics include statistical supervised and unsupervised learning methods, randomized search algorithms, and Bayesian learning methods, as well as theoretical concepts such as inductive bias, the PAC and Mistake-bound learning frameworks, the minimum description length principle, and Ockham's Razor. This term emphasizes advanced topics to bring the material closer to the state of the art, with programming and practical application across a number of projects.

## Objectives

There are four primary objectives:

- Provide foundational knowledge with a broad survey of approaches and techniques in ML.
- Develop a deeper understanding of several major topics in ML.
- Develop the design and programming skills to build intelligent, adaptive artifacts.
- Develop necessary skills to communicate research and practicum in ML.

The last bullet is the core objective and separates this offering from many others.

## Prerequisites

## Scoring and Grading

The final grade is determined by a reading/writing quiz, hypothesis report practice, 3 unit quizzes, 3 comprehensive reports, and a cumulative final exam.

- **Reading/Writing Quiz.** A lecture and quiz to establish formal report reading/writing skills. Unlimited attempts until the due date. Available at the start of week 1, due end of week 2.
- **Hypothesis Report Practice.** An exercise on hypothesis interpretation and development, with feedback before the first assignment. Available end of week 1, due end of week 2.
- **Unit Quizzes.** One quiz per unit (SL, UL, RL). Each unit is three weeks; the quiz opens in week 2 of the unit and is open until the end of week 3. A mix of selection and calculation questions drawn randomly from a test bank; 60 minutes total, 3 attempts over the window, correct/incorrect shown after each attempt. One sheet of notes and a calculator allowed; closed-book, closed-internet. Practice questions posted on Ed Discussions beforehand.
- **Reports.** Three scored reports — Supervised Learning (SL), Unsupervised Learning (UL), and Reinforcement Learning (RL) — covering programming and analysis. Reports must be written in LaTeX on Overleaf; code must use Private Georgia Tech GitHub repos (personal GitHub is prohibited and unscored). No rubric is provided (by pedagogical design), though an extensive internal rubric, FAQs, twice-weekly office hours, an Ed forum, example exemplary conference papers, low-stakes practice quizzes, and detailed TA feedback support each assignment.
- **Reviewer Response** *(change for Summer 2025).* Within one week of a grade being posted, submit a two-page-maximum response with edits made and reviewer feedback. A proper Reviewer Response needs the initial submission, revised submission, and two-page response. If satisfactory, half the missed points are returned (e.g., 70/100 → 85/100).
- **Final Exam.** A digital, closed-book final exam administered via Canvas and Honorlock.
- **Final Letter Grade.** Grades are calculated on a curve at the instructor's discretion (no other assignments are curved). A majority of students typically fall in the A and B ranges, though exact cutoffs vary by term.

*Note:* a midterm administered in previous semesters has been removed. This term introduces three new quizzes and the Reviewer Response, so assignment averages and overall grades are expected to shift slightly.

## Due Dates

All scored assignments are due at the indicated Eastern Time (ET); Canvas does not support Anywhere On Earth. Assignments are due at 11:59:00 PM ET on the date due, with an official late mark at 7:59:00 AM ET (students should treat 11:59 PM as the internal deadline).

For **reports only**, late assignments incur a 20%-off-the-top per-day penalty for a maximum of 5 days, or a 0 grade. Exceptions require (1) a note from the appropriate authority and (2) immediate notification with a proper explanation. Approved late submissions are accepted only up to one full week after the due date; after that, a 0 is automatic. For **quizzes**, since there are multiple attempts, each quiz is due at the exact due date. Preset due dates are maintained except for widespread outside forces preventing use of required technology; any change comes with at least 24-hour notice.
